# A lobby you can walk through, and guards that finally moved

## From Nico

Today was our first working day since we settled on the main idea after the kickoff. I'm very happy with how much the AI harness is making our work easier. It lets us work in parallel without stepping on each other, because it keeps a record of pending tasks, and we ask for every requirement in natural language, just as we planned. The only manual work left is configuration inside the editor, such as the guards' routes or the level layout itself, which leaves us time to make the most of our creativity.

## What changed

Since the last entry we set out to turn the story state into something you can play: a first section, a player who can sneak, and a guard who catches you.

By the end of the stretch the core was in. Pablo built the story state subsystem, the detection component (view cone, line trace, and a meter that fills slower when the player crouches), a patrolling guard, a sweeping camera and the capture flow: "NO, WAIT...", lose a beer, reload at the checkpoint, and "They left the bar" at zero beers. He then added choosing a story correction after capture, the EMP item that disables a nearby camera for a while, and an office exit that opens with the lockpick or a story key. The smoke item has started. Nicolas made the three section maps, walk, sprint and crouch (crouching fits a 120 cm vent), and a colour-coded greybox of the section 1 lobby: grey structure, blue cover, green entrances, yellow exits, orange vents, red guard and camera spots. He also built the entry and item main menu fully in C++ UMG, so all three entrances are playable.

Ivan moved the art from tests to real kits. He built the bar where the story is told: 18 models, their materials and the L_Bar map with the room laid out, plus the money bag prop that tested the pipeline. Then he built the bank's ground floor kit in Blender through its MCP server: 150 low-poly comic meshes (shell modules, stairs, doors, furniture, ATMs, props, utilities, plaza and street) generated by team-written scripts, with a shared palette of materials and a placement guide. All of it is original work and recorded in the asset ledger.

The worst bug was invisible. Guards never moved. Their collision sweep hit the floor they stood on, and the unit test passed anyway because it spawned the guard in an empty world. We fixed it with a sweep that ignores the floor and added a regression test that puts the guard on a floor. While there, we exposed per-point wait, facing, turn speed and Loop or PingPong routes in the Details panel, so each placed guard is configured by hand in the editor.

Working through the editor agent cost us time in other ways:

- Clicking File > New Level through the editor's MCP opened a modal dialog that froze every editor call. We now duplicate the engine template map and save before opening.
- A save-all once re-saved an unrelated Blueprint, so we check every save against git status.
- One CI run failed only because the self-hosted runner is a teammate's PC, and Live Coding in his open editor blocks external builds.
- Parallel work collided twice in `docs/design/tasks.md`. We kept both rows each time.
- A teammate reported that exits did nothing. We added logs, which showed the trigger working on the builder's machine. That pointed to a missing local compile on the tester's side.

We asked for the work in plain language and agents did the C++, Unreal Editor and Blender steps. The editor configuration, such as guard routes and the level layout, we kept for ourselves. Ivan playtested the greybox and Pablo confirmed the level transition, which is how we checked that someone other than the builder could finish it.

One number sets the next job. Our target for a full run of the lobby is 2 to 4 minutes, and today's runs take about 30 seconds, with two guards and a camera placed. The level is too small, so we decided to enlarge it and redistribute guards and cameras (Level design, Gameplay). We colour-coded the greybox by role, from entrances to guard spots (Level design). We built the menu so all three entrances are playable (Gameplay), and correction after capture is already in, so the theme is in the loop (Theme).
